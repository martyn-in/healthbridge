WORLD HEALTH ORGANIZATION (WHO) - GUIDELINES FOR THE CLINICAL MANAGEMENT OF SNAKEBITES

DOCUMENT SUMMARY:
Authoritative WHO emergency protocol for venomous snakebite first-aid and pre-hospital emergency management.

1. IMMEDIATE FIRST-AID STEPS FOR SNAKEBITE:
- Immediate Actions:
  1. Keep the victim calm and reassure them. Anxiety speeds up heart rate and accelerates venom systemic absorption.
  2. Immediately immobilize the affected limb using a splint or sling, maintaining the bitten limb below or at heart level. Restrict all physical movement of the victim completely.
  3. Remove rings, bangles, watches, tight clothing, or shoes from the bitten limb immediately before localized swelling develops.
  4. Transport the victim immediately and safely to the nearest health facility equipped with anti-snake venom (ASV). Call 112 / 108 emergency transport immediately.
  5. Try to note the snake's color, size, and head shape from a safe distance if possible, but NEVER attempt to catch or kill the snake.

2. DANGEROUS PRACTICES TO AVOID (STRICT WHO CONTRAINDICATIONS):
- What to Avoid (STRICTLY PROHIBITED):
  1. Do NOT apply a tight tourniquet, arterial band, or rope above the bite site. This causes severe localized tissue ischemia, gangrene, and amputation.
  2. Do NOT cut, incise, or slash the bite wound.
  3. Do NOT suck the wound (by mouth or mechanical suction device).
  4. Do NOT apply ice packs, cold compresses, chemical pastes, herbs, cow dung, or electrical shocks to the bite area.
  5. Do NOT give the victim alcohol, sedatives, aspirin, or oral pain medication.

3. WARNING SIGNS & SYSTEMIC ENVENOMATION SYMPTOMS:
- Local Signs: Rapid swelling spreading up the limb, severe localized pain, blistering, bruising, active bleeding from puncture marks.
- Systemic Neurotoxic Signs (Cobra / Krait): Ptosis (drooping eyelids), difficulty swallowing, slurred speech, progressive respiratory muscle paralysis, muscle weakness.
- Systemic Hemotoxic Signs (Viper): Uncontrolled bleeding from gums or previous wounds, vomiting blood, dark urine (hemoglobinuria), sudden drop in blood pressure, shock.

4. CRITICAL CARE NOTE:
Immediate administration of polyvalent Anti-Snake Venom (ASV) at a hospital emergency room is the only definitive treatment for systemic snake envenomation. Pre-hospital first-aid must focus exclusively on immobilization and emergency transport.
