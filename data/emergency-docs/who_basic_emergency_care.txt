WORLD HEALTH ORGANIZATION (WHO) - BASIC EMERGENCY CARE GUIDELINES FOR FIRST RESPONDERS

DOCUMENT SUMMARY:
Official first-aid and emergency management procedures established by the World Health Organization (WHO) for acute trauma, respiratory compromise, poisoning, burns, and cardiac events.

1. AIRWAY AND BREATHING EMERGENCY (RESPIRATORY DISTRESS / CHOKING):
- Immediate Actions:
  1. Position the patient upright if conscious. If unconscious and breathing normally, place in the recovery position (side-lying).
  2. If choking (conscious adult/child): Perform up to 5 back blows between the shoulder blades followed by up to 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich maneuver).
  3. If patient stops breathing or becomes unresponsive, immediately call Emergency Medical Services (112 / 108) and begin CPR (30 chest compressions followed by 2 breaths at a rate of 100-120 compressions per minute).
- What to Avoid (Dangerous Practices):
  1. Do NOT perform blind finger sweeps in the mouth.
  2. Do NOT give water or fluids to an unconscious or heavily gasping patient.
- Warning Signs:
  Cyanosis (bluish skin/lips), inability to speak or cough, stridor (high-pitched gasping sound), chest wall indrawing.

2. SEVERE BLEEDING AND HEMORRHAGE CONTROL:
- Immediate Actions:
  1. Apply firm, direct pressure over the bleeding wound using a clean cloth, sterile gauze, or gloved hand.
  2. Elevate the injured limb above heart level if possible and if no fracture is suspected.
  3. Maintain continuous pressure for at least 10 to 15 minutes without lifting the pad to inspect the wound.
  4. Secure with a pressure bandage. If blood soaks through, apply additional pads over the top without removing the initial dressing.
  5. Call Emergency Services (112 / 108) immediately for heavy, arterial, or uncontrolled bleeding.
- What to Avoid:
  1. Do NOT apply improvised tourniquets unless trained and direct pressure completely fails to control life-threatening arterial bleeding.
  2. Do NOT remove embedded foreign objects (knives, glass) from wounds; stabilize them in place with bulky padding.
- Warning Signs:
  Rapid weak pulse, pale clammy skin, confusion, dizziness, hypovolemic shock.

3. BURNS (THERMAL AND CHEMICAL):
- Immediate Actions:
  1. Cool the burn immediately with clean, cool running tap water for at least 10 to 20 minutes.
  2. Remove non-adherent clothing and jewelry around the burned area before swelling occurs.
  3. Cover the burn loosely with a clean, non-stick sterile bandage or clean plastic wrap.
  4. Call 112 / 108 for burns involving the face, hands, feet, groin, major joints, or burns covering >10% of total body surface area.
- What to Avoid:
  1. Do NOT apply ice, ice water, butter, oil, toothpaste, or home remedies to burns.
  2. Do NOT pop blistered skin.
  3. Do NOT peel off clothing that is stuck to burned skin.
- Warning Signs:
  Charred white or black skin, blistering over large areas, difficulty breathing if facial burns or smoke inhalation occurred.

4. ACUTE POISONING AND TOXIC INGESTION:
- Immediate Actions:
  1. Determine the identity of the substance, quantity ingested, and time of exposure if safe to do so.
  2. Immediately contact emergency medical assistance (112 / 108) or local Poison Control Center.
  3. If poison was on skin or in eyes, flush continuously with clean water for 15-20 minutes.
- What to Avoid:
  1. Do NOT induce vomiting unless specifically instructed by medical personnel or poison control.
  2. Do NOT give raw eggs, vinegar, or home concoctions to neutralize chemical poisons.
- Warning Signs:
  Drowsiness, vomiting, burns around lips/mouth, strange breath odor, seizures, difficulty breathing.
